# One Step Between Yes and Booked

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapters 9 and 11 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=3414s and https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=4231s)

## What Nick teaches

The fourth step of the framework is a **specific ask with a specific time**. Not "would you be interested?" or "let me know your thoughts."

His CTA: "Would you be open to a 15-minute chat? If so, I can give you a ring at 3:30pm today or before 12pm tomorrow." The loop is send, they say yes, you book. Two steps.

Contrast the common version: "let me know thoughts" then "cool" then "want a call?" then "sure" then "what time?" then "3pm Thursday" then "can't, soccer practice." Every back and forth leaks about 5% of the funnel, and five of them lose 25% of your lead flow, which is 25% of your revenue. "Entire industries are built on 25%."

Friction reducers that belong in the CTA:
- Name the length ("15 minutes").
- Name two concrete times.
- Give the default action path: "I can give you a ring at <their number> or send you a one-click Google Meet invite." If the number is wrong they correct it. If the time is wrong they propose another. Either way they are already in the action path.
- State how little else is required: "We won't have to talk again until all 20 meetings are delivered."
- For asset-based goals: "It'll be a one-click link, no download, about 30 seconds. Want me to send it?"
- Sign off with a first name. Nick likes a simple "Thanks, Nick."

Cold email platforms support **liquid-style variables** like `{{first_name}}` and `{{phone}}` so the CTA can carry their actual number.

Nick's CTA variants from the roasts:
- "Could show you how it works over a casual 15-minute call. Can ring you 10am today or Thursday?"
- "Any interest? Could show you in around 15 minutes. Can call today around noon or tomorrow 3:30pm if that's easier, or I'll send a Google Meet link."
- "Are you opposed to a 15 to 30 minute chat in the next few days? I know you're on ET time. Can give you a ring at 3:30pm today or tomorrow."
- Low-friction reply CTAs: "Just send me the title and some bullets." "Just send me the raw footage in a Google Drive link with sharing on." "Just reply yes."

## Step by step

1. Confirm the goal from ce-campaign-goal-and-frame (reply, watch, or book). Write the CTA for that goal only.
2. For book: state length, two times (today and tomorrow, or two days), and how you will reach them.
3. For watch: say it is one click, no download, and how long it takes. Ask whether to send it.
4. For reply: ask for one word or one small artifact ("reply yes," "send me the address").
5. Add the "that's all you'll have to do" line if there is any chance they think it is more work.
6. Sign off with your first name only.
7. Count the steps between their "yes" and the booked outcome. If it is more than one, rewrite.

## Templates / prompts

```
Book:   "Open to a 15-minute chat? I can ring you at {{phone}} at {{time1}} today or {{time2}} tomorrow, or send a one-click invite."
Watch:  "I recorded you a 40-second walkthrough. Want me to send it? One click, nothing to download."
Reply:  "If you want the number, just reply 'yes' and I'll have it to you by {{day}}."
Visit:  "I can swing by for 10 minutes Saturday at 10 or Sunday at 2. Which is easier?"
```

Real estate examples:
```
"Open to 15 minutes at the house? I can come by Thursday at 4:15 or Saturday at 10, or just call you at {{phone}} first if you'd rather."
"Reply 'yes' and I'll drop the one-page value range in your mailbox Thursday. That's genuinely all you have to do."
```

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Vague asks ("interested?", "thoughts?") that start a scheduling negotiation.
- Asking for a "quick chat" with no length. Could be 30 minutes, could be an hour.
- Making them pick the time from nothing. Offer two.
- Links in cold email, SMS, and most DMs tank deliverability and read as scam. Prefer "want me to send it?" over pasting the link.
- Pricing in the CTA.

## How Caleb should use this

- Every AIRE draft ends with one of the four CTA patterns, filled with Caleb's real availability from his calendar. Sending is "needs Caleb's approval before running."
- Pull two open slots from Google Calendar when drafting so the times are real, and hold them until the prospect responds.
- For door-knock or mailer follow-up, use the visit CTA with two specific windows.
- Use the "reply yes" CTA for SMS and first-touch mailers where a call is too big an ask.

## Verification

- CTA names a length, two times, and the contact method, or asks for a one-word reply.
- No link in the first message unless the goal is "watch" and the asset is described first.
- Steps from "yes" to booked = 1.
