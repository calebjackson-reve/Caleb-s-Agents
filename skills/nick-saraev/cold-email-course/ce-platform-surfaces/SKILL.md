# Platform Surfaces and Character Budgets

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapters 23 to 25 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=10203s)

## What Nick teaches

Take the four-step message and "massage it into the shape" of each platform. People judge you on every visible element before they read a word of body copy, and most senders only optimize the subject line. Nick enumerates every lever. Character counts are his estimates from his own screen and vary by device.

**Cold email**
- Sender name (~20 chars). Use first and last name. It signals origin and legitimacy.
- Subject line (~30 to 50 chars) and teaser (~50 to 100 chars) share about **148 to 150 characters** combined in the inbox row. A short subject leaves more teaser room. Unused space gets filled with metadata ("On Tuesday, March 17th..."), so write at least 150 characters total and fill the budget.
- After opening: email address, profile picture, recency, internal vs external label. All judged.
- Then the body.
- Checklist: is this the best name, subject, teaser, picture, address, body?

**LinkedIn**
- Before opening: profile picture (priority one, smiling, good lighting, strong foreground vs background contrast), first name (shorter first names leave more teaser room), teaser ~53 to 55 chars.
- After opening: title (~50 to 60 chars), picture again, Premium badge (gold, makes you stand out), credential line (for example "HubSpot Elite Partner," a master's degree), time, body.
- Advice: professional picture, get Premium plus certifications to add to the top of your message and reduce landing in the "Other" inbox. LinkedIn and email share a similar tone, slightly more formal, but shorter casual DMs can be a pattern interrupt.

**X (Twitter)**
- Message requests are the default for strangers. The whole game is making it out of requests.
- Visible: profile picture, display name (often not a real full name), teaser ~40 to 55 chars, handle (a silly handle is discordant), join date (brand new accounts look like spam, aged accounts needed for scale), body (long bodies display in full), gifts (verified followers only).
- Tone: casual, lowercase, emojis, sarcasm. Write in the same tone of voice the platform uses.

**Instagram**
- Also message requests. Visible: picture, handle (can differ from display name), teaser ~30 chars, time, platform of origin (Facebook vs Instagram), body. Accept, delete, or block.
- Tone: casual, same as X.

**iMessage / SMS**
- Visible: picture, phone number, teaser ~90 chars (the preview is the body for short texts), SMS vs iMessage label (blue bubbles get more trust), body. iMessage can send photos, stickers, effects.
- Advice: write at least 1.5x the preview length and put something provocative or curiosity-driving right near the end of the preview so they must open it. A message that fits entirely in the preview can be dismissed without a tap.

Universal rule: fill the teaser, bury a hook near its end so the click is required. Same idea as subject line plausible deniability.

## Step by step

1. Start from the finished four-step email.
2. Email: write sender name, subject, and first body line so that subject + first ~100 chars total ~150 and the hook sits at the end of the teaser.
3. LinkedIn: tighten line one to ~50 chars with the hook. Check the sender profile (picture, title, badge, credential) before sending anything.
4. X and Instagram: compress to a casual 2 to 4 line DM, hook in the first 30 to 50 chars, no links. Expect to land in requests, so line one must earn the accept.
5. SMS: write ~130 to 180 characters, curiosity line at character ~80 to 90, no link in the first text.
6. Check sender assets on every platform: name, photo, handle, age of account, title.
7. Log character counts next to each variant.


Real estate SMS example (hook near the preview boundary):
```
Hey Dana, Caleb here, I'm the one who sold the brick place two doors down on Royal. Pulled a number on yours this morning and honestly it surprised me. Want it? Reply yes and I'll text it over.
```

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Leaving the subject and teaser budget empty so metadata fills it.
- Weak LinkedIn photo with no contrast, no badge, no credential.
- New X or Instagram accounts and odd handles that read as bots.
- Entire SMS visible in the preview, so no reason to open.
- Links in any first-touch DM or text.
- Ignoring that LinkedIn, X, and Instagram all have request inboxes for strangers.

## How Caleb should use this

- AIRE should produce the platform matrix for every campaign so Caleb approves one message set, not five separate drafts. Any send is "needs Caleb's approval before running."
- Sender assets for Caleb: full name on email, a well-lit headshot on every platform, LinkedIn title that reads human ("Realtor in St. Francisville and Baton Rouge, Keller Williams First Choice"), and credentials he actually holds.
- SMS to consumers is heavily regulated (see ce-compliant-outbound-guardrails). Use the SMS budget rules for texts to people who have given consent or an existing relationship, and for follow-ups after a reply.
- For Instagram DMs to local business owners (referral partners, vendors), expect the request inbox and lead with the local hook.
