# The Four-Step Cold Email Framework

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapters 5, 6 and 9 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=2094s)

## What Nick teaches

Every piece of outbound answers four questions the reader asks in order, and each step answers exactly one:

| Step | Reader's question | What you write | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Personalization | Is this a scammer or spammer? | Greeting + an observation or thing in common that segues into the pitch. Must not signal selling. | 1 sentence ideal, 2 max |
| 2. Who am I and why does it matter | OK, so who are you and why should I care? | Social proof as your introduction: "I currently work with <client in their reference group> to do <thing like what they do>. We did <specific number> in <timeframe>." | 1 to 2 sentences |
| 3. Offer | What can you do for me? | An observation about their situation (often cold-readable) plus a too-good-to-be-true offer with built-in risk reversal: "I will do X in Y time or Z risk mitigation." | 2 to 4 sentences |
| 4. CTA | What do you want me to do? | One specific ask with a specific time. "Open to a 15-minute chat? I can ring you at 3:30pm today or before noon tomorrow." Then sign off. | 1 to 2 sentences |

The first lines are the highest ROI real estate in the email because drop-off is brutal: 100% read the first word, maybe 50% read the fifth. So the personalization has to hook. It is usually built with **cold reading**: general statements that apply to most of the audience but feel specific (see ce-cold-read-opener).

**Voluntary disclosure of information.** In the personalization, give away something about yourself ("helped me get started in management consulting"). People trust someone who shares first. Nick credits this to FBI rapport technique.

**Social proof is your intro.** Do not say "I'm a thumbnail designer." Who you are is the people you have worked with and the results you got. Borrow the credibility of the client. If the client is in their in-group, you have signaled in-group membership too.

**Offer uses risk reversal.** The prospect should risk nothing. "I'll generate you $10K in 60 days or keep working free until I do." No ranges ("10 to 20K"). Exact numbers, exact timeframe. Full formula in ce-offer-formula.

**CTA is one step from yes to booked.** Full detail in ce-one-step-cta.

His full template (pitching a YouTuber on landing pages):

```
Hey Nick, love your channel man. Very no-BS and has helped me get started in management consulting. Think I can help you with something and maybe return a bit of the favor you've unwittingly done me.

I currently work with a 10M-sub YouTuber, Mr. X, to help him build landing pages. We've made $3M in the last month alone.

I went through your landing page at Maker School and frankly you are bleeding money, my friend. I'm so confident even a couple minor changes could fix this that I bet I could generate at least $100K for you in the next 60 days. I'd do this 100% upfront, no strings, it would take 5 minutes of your time, and only if I hit $100K would I ask for a small cap, maybe 15 to 20%.

Would you be open to a 15-minute call? If so, 3:30pm tomorrow?
Peter
```

The Maker School rewrite he shows (shorter, punchier, with a deliberate typo at the end):

```
Hi Nick, love the channel man. The anti-hype is very refreshing. Honestly wanted to run something by you.

I help retail businesses scale acquisition. Just partnered with Venshin, a retail company around the same size as you, and generated $85K rev in 12 weeks.

Could I do this for you too? TLDR: I'd fill your calendar with high-intent sales calls from prospects genuinely interested in your offer. I do it with a scalable cold outreach system I built myself. I believe in this so strongly I wouldn't charge a cent unless it generated more than $10K in our first month together. My goal is to make this a no-brainer.

Would you be able to do a 15-minute call about this? If so, 3:30pm tomorrow or 4pm Tuesday, I can send over a cal invite. Just gve me a shout.
Thanks, Miguel
```

Nick says 80 to 90% of situations fit this formula, across email, LinkedIn, SMS, even snail mail with a swag box. Learn the rules before you bend them.

## Step by step

1. Confirm the one-sentence campaign goal (ce-campaign-goal-and-frame).
2. Write step 1: greeting plus one cold-readable observation or shared thing. Add one voluntary disclosure. Check it does not sound like selling.
3. Write step 2: name a client or describe one ("a brick ranch owner in Zachary"), a result with a specific number, a timeframe. Make the client look like the reader.
4. Write step 3: one observation about their situation, then "I will do X in Y time or Z." No ranges, no hedging.
5. Write step 4: one ask, one or two specific times, how you will reach them. Sign off with first name only.
6. Read the whole thing as a text message. Cut every sentence that is not doing one of the four jobs.
7. Score with ce-seven-trust-principles. Fix any X.

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Personalization that sounds like an LLM ("love how passionate you are about process optimization"). Nobody says that.
- Introducing yourself by job title instead of by results and clients.
- Ranges and hedges in the offer. "I'll make you 10 to 20K" loses to "I'll make you $20K in 90 days."
- Vague CTAs: "let me know your thoughts," "would you be interested."
- Breaking the formula before you understand it.

## How Caleb should use this

- Default skeleton for AIRE's first-touch drafts to expireds, FSBOs, absentee owners, and investors. Every outbound draft is "needs Caleb's approval before running."
- Step 2 for Caleb: "I'm with Keller Williams First Choice here in St. Francisville. Sold the Hebert place on Commerce St in 9 days at 2% over list this spring." Specific street, specific number, same price band as the reader.
- Step 3 offers that a Louisiana licensee can honestly make: a written home value range delivered in 48 hours, a buyer-activity report, a 30-day marketing plan preview, a staging walkthrough at no cost. Never promise a sale price or a sale in a set time (see ce-compliant-outbound-guardrails).
- Step 4 for Caleb: "I can call you at 4:15 today or swing by Saturday at 10." Local and concrete.
- Keep a library of each step's best-performing lines in the CRM so new campaigns are assembled, not reinvented.

## Verification

- Each of the four steps can be pointed to in the draft, and nothing else remains.
- Step 1 contains no selling language and would pass as a note from an acquaintance.
- Step 2 has a number. Step 3 has a deliverable, a timeframe, and a risk reversal. Step 4 has a time.
- Whole message reads as a text from one person.
