# Roast and Rewrite

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapters 14 to 22 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=5387s)

## What Nick teaches

Nick opens his inbox, pastes ten real cold pitches into a doc titled "Cold Email Roasts: Before and After," scores each on the seven principles, then rewrites it in the four-step framework. Three things can be improved on any email: the sender name, the subject line, and the first ~100 characters (the teaser). Then the body.

He never claims a rewrite is proven, because he cannot test against the original. "Don't listen to your opinions. Listen to what the market tells you."

**Failure patterns he found, and the fix:**

| What he saw | Why it fails | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Hi Nick's Daily Updates" / "Hi Nick Automates" | Raw scraped channel name in greeting | First word of the name field only |
| Quoted or bolded video title in the body | Formatting proves it was scraped | No formatting on variables, or reuse a generic in-group reference ("your last Claude Code video") |
| "Email tracked with Mailsuite" footer, opt-out lines | Screams mass tool | Remove. He does not add opt-outs for legitimate B2B contacts (see compliance skill for Caleb's situation) |
| "Happy to show you over a casual 15-min call" as the give | A call takes time, it gives nothing | Give a real asset: build it, audit it, send credits |
| "I'm a thumbnail designer" | Job title is not identity | "I make thumbnails for a few creators at 1 to 2M subs" |
| "I'm Grove, an AI COO running a 10-agent system" | Branding yourself a robot kills authority and identity | Write as a human with a name |
| $500 and $25,500 prices in the email | Price is not a micro commitment | Never put pricing in a cold email |
| Links to landing pages | Spam signal, deliverability hit | Cut all links. Describe the asset, offer to send it |
| "Most creators lose viewers in the first 60 seconds" | Reads like a TV commercial, general and vague | "Nick, YOU are losing a tremendous number of viewers in the first 60 seconds right now" |
| Three dense lines, no breaks | No breathability | Short paragraphs |
| "We support companies refine their value proposition, align marketing and sales handoffs" | PhD jargon, "we" voice | "I build sales systems for $5 to 10M marketing companies. I help you figure out CAC, LTV and drop-offs, then fix the leaks" |
| "Quick chat in the coming days" | Vague ask, vague length | "15 to 30 minute chat, I can ring you 3:30 today or tomorrow" |
| "On behalf of our founder Harris, our other co-founder made you a video..." | Five people, too long, offer buried at bottom | One sender, offer moved to top, video linked bluntly once |
| Survey link, "part 1 of 3," 15 to 20 minutes | Terrifying commitment | "5 minutes," remove progress labels, give $50 credits first |
| "Small AI team" | Downplayed social proof | "$10M+ AI team in San Fran" |
| "Could be good content for your audience" | Asks a creator to do free work, shows no shared identity | Align incentives: "I think this would improve the lives of many of your followers" |
| Subject "Paid collaboration: Runnable" | Corporate, no name | "Hey Nick, Runnable wants to send you money" |
| Three identical follow-ups back to back | Annoying, spam risk | One short human ping (see follow-up skill) |

**Moves he adds in most rewrites:**
- A casual qualifier in line one ("man," "lol," "btw").
- One voluntary disclosure ("my GF says I listen to you more than her").
- Social proof with a number in the reference group.
- "I'm not here to pitch you on paying me" when the offer is a free deliverable.
- "I want to put my money where my mouth is" before the offer.
- "I know this is the first you're hearing of me and you may not be comfortable working with a stranger, but..." to acknowledge cold.
- "Mostly on commission" or "results basis" to signal aligned incentives. Nick notes you only need 51% of comp to be variable to say "mostly."
- A deliberate small typo late in the email, never early.
- Offer moved near the top when the email must be long.
- Scarcity, which he often forgets, added as real capacity ("in talks with a few other podcasts," "anytime this week").

Rewrites often come out longer than the original because the originals had no concrete offer. Length is not the goal, completeness is.

## Step by step

1. Paste the original with sender name, subject, and teaser visible. Note what the reader sees before opening.
2. Score 0 to 7 with ce-seven-trust-principles, one line of reason per principle.
3. Run the failure table above. List each hit.
4. Rewrite in the ce-four-step-framework. Keep the sender's real facts. Where a fact is missing (a case study, a number), insert a clearly marked placeholder and tell the sender what they need to source.
5. Fix subject and teaser with ce-subject-line-deniability.
6. Re-score. Show before and after side by side.
7. State plainly that the rewrite is a hypothesis to be tested, not a guarantee.

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Grading your own rewrite 100%. He gives himself 7 or 8 out of 10 and says so.
- Inventing case studies. He uses placeholders and says the sender must have the real thing, though he notes most people can turn real experience into a case study with a couple hours of work.
- Making everything longer. Only add length when the original had no offer.
- Rewriting the whole thing with AI. Use the human framework, then spot check.
