# Campaign Goal and Human Frame

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapter 4 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=1113s)

## What Nick teaches

A great outbound campaign has three components: a clear goal, a frame, and iteration. Most people skip the first one entirely.

**1. Define the goal and KPIs.** Nick treats every single message as a self-contained campaign. If you stripped away every other touch, that one message should do the whole job. The goal is "the one thing I want them to do after reading this message." He gives four buckets, and says he almost only uses the first three:
- Reply (easiest, "reply yes and I'll send it")
- Watch or click something (a bigger ask, they leave the inbox)
- Book a call (big ask, but closest to money, the default for services)
- Buy (rare, he does not put pricing in cold emails)

Each goal is written differently. The fewer steps between reading and doing, the better, because "leads leak" at every back and forth. For a call: propose the exact time and offer to ring them. For a watch: "one click, no download, 30 seconds."

Rule: **if you cannot describe the goal in one sentence, you are not ready to write the campaign.** Example of a good one: "Book calls for a B2B outbound lead gen offer."

KPIs he tracks as a funnel: opens (if visible), replies, opt-ins (next step taken), calls, proposals, closes, and lifetime value tied back to the campaign. Metrics vary by platform.

**2. Build the frame: one to one, human.** Cold outbound is one person to one person. Even when you use templates, the reader must believe you wrote it just for them. The vibe: "I was on the sidewalk calling an Uber, saw a notification about your business, and had to message you right now."

Tests:
- **Text message test.** If a friend saw you typing this, would they think it was a personal message or a mass email? Go look at how you actually text people you trust and mirror that tone.
- **Kill corporate signals.** No "hope this finds you well." No big signature block. Say "I" not "we." Keep it short, casual, slightly imperfect. Nick even adds "Sent from my iPhone" style imperfections so it reads as human.
- **Read-back test.** If this landed in my inbox, would I think it was a blast or a note written for me?

**3. Iterate.** Treat it like the scientific method: hypothesis (this email books calls), send, measure, change, loop. Send 500 to 1,000 per variant to get statistical backing. Kill losers fast, build new variants off winners. Data beats gut, and revealed preference (what customers do) beats stated preference (what they say). ## Step by step

1. Write the goal in one sentence. Name the single action: reply, watch, or book. If it takes two sentences, keep cutting.
2. Pick the KPI funnel for that platform and list what you can actually measure (email: open, reply, booked, proposal, close. SMS: reply, booked. DM: reply, accepted request).
3. Decide the reader. One person, one role. Not "homeowners," but "absentee owner of a single family rental in Zachary who has owned 7+ years."
4. Draft in the frame: open your own text thread with a friend and write in that voice. First person singular. No greeting-card openers.
5. Run the three tests (text message, corporate signals, read back). Fix anything that fails.
6. Decide the minimum send volume per variant and the review cadence before sending anything.

## Templates / prompts

```
CAMPAIGN BRIEF
Goal (one sentence): Get absentee owners in East Baton Rouge Parish to reply "yes" to a free rent-vs-sell number.
Action asked for: reply
Reader: one absentee owner, 7+ years of ownership, out of state
Channel: letter + follow-up text
KPIs: delivered, replies, numbers sent, listing appointments, listings taken, closed, GCI
Volume per variant: 500
Review cadence: every Sunday, 30 minutes
Frame check: text message test [ ] corporate signals removed [ ] read-back [ ]
```

Prompt for Claude when drafting:
```
Write this as a text from one person to one person. First person singular. Under 120 words.
No "hope this finds you well", no signature block, no "we". One ask, stated with a specific time or a one-word reply.
Then run the text message test and tell me honestly whether a friend would think this was a mass email.
```

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Running outbound because it is trendy, without knowing why. "If you don't know why you're running a campaign there's no point running it."
- Vague goals like "get interest" that make every follow-up a negotiation about next steps.
- "We" language and polished signatures that scream company-to-prospect instead of person-to-person.
- Trusting intuition over data. Campaigns he was sure would flop hit 15% reply rates.

## How Caleb should use this

- Before AIRE sends anything, it should produce a one-sentence goal and a KPI list. Any send is "needs Caleb's approval before running."
- Expired and withdrawn listing campaigns: goal is usually "book a 15-minute listing consult at a stated time." FSBO: goal is "reply yes to a free buyer-activity report."
- Investor outreach (off-market deals, rental portfolios): goal is "reply" first, then escalate. Never pricing or commission terms in the first message.
- Keep Caleb's frame local and human: how he would text a neighbor in St. Francisville, not how a brokerage newsletter reads.
- Log every campaign's goal and KPIs in the CRM so Sunday iteration has a baseline.

## Verification

- The goal fits in one sentence and names exactly one action.
- Each draft passes the text message test when read aloud.
- A KPI sheet exists before the first send, with volume per variant and a review day.
