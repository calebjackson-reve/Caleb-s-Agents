# Two-Touch Follow-Up, Then Earn the Third

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapter 27 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=12127s)

## What Nick teaches

People overdo follow-ups ("20 a day") and overcomplicate them (case studies, third-cousin references). Nick's practice on any platform:

1. **Start with two messages.** The initial email and one simple follow-up. One follow-up already puts you ahead of 99.9% of senders who do none.
2. **Follow-ups are pings, not newsletters.** Real humans who spent effort on the first message would write: "Hey Pete, are you cool man? Get back to me." "Hey Pete, checking in on X, let me know if you have questions." "Hey Pete, let me know if this got lost in your mailbox." Not: "While analyzing LeftClick I noticed you were spending XYZ on platforms, our recent case study AAA Corp saved over $10,000..."
3. **Reformulate the initial chunk and change the subject.** "Hey X, checking in on Y. TLDR if this is the first time you're seeing it: <short restatement>. Thanks, Nick." A different subject each time lets you test subjects repeatedly.
4. **Why only two at first:** you do not yet know if the campaign is good. Every extra step in an unproven sequence multiplies the chance of spam/block (email), delete/archive (LinkedIn), decline (X, Instagram). Those actions damage the asset: your domain, your phone numbers, your profiles.
5. **Add steps only when overperforming.** His example: 2% reply rate with two emails, do not add. 4.8% with two emails, add a third and expect ~6.1%. Add a fourth and expect ~6.8%. More touchpoints lift replies, but only once the base sequence has earned it.

## Step by step

1. Build the sequence as exactly two touches. Space them a few days apart (Nick does not give a fixed gap, keep it human).
2. Write the follow-up as 2 to 4 lines: casual ping, one-line TLDR of the original offer, sign off. New subject.
3. Send 500 to 1,000 per variant (see ce-sunday-iteration) and track reply rate and block or spam rate.
4. If reply rate is clearly above your baseline, add a third touch (another human ping, maybe a new angle on the same offer). Measure again.
5. Keep adding one step at a time while replies rise and complaints stay flat. Stop the moment complaints rise.
6. Anyone who replies, even "no," exits the sequence immediately.

## Templates / prompts

```
Touch 1: full four-step message (ce-four-step-framework)
Touch 2 (new subject, 3 days later):
  Subject: {{first}}, did this get buried?
  Hey {{first}}, checking in on the note below. TLDR if it's the first time you're seeing it: {{one-line offer}}. Still happy to {{tiny ask}} this week. 
  Caleb
Touch 3 (only if touch 1+2 overperform, new subject):
  Subject: {{street}} one more time
  Hey {{first}}, last ping from me. {{one new reason or a real capacity note}}. If the timing's wrong just say so and I'll leave you be.
  Caleb
```

Real estate follow-up text (after a mailer or email, where texting is permitted):
```
Hey {{first}}, Caleb again. Did the one-pager on {{street}} make it to you? Happy to walk you through it in 5 min if useful.
```

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Long, newsletter-style follow-ups with case studies.
- Three identical pitches back to back (the "Grove" sender did this and it killed any chance).
- Scaling to five or six steps before the two-step version is proven.
- Reusing the same subject.
- Letting replied contacts keep receiving the sequence.

## How Caleb should use this

- CRM drips for expireds and FSBOs should launch as two touches, not the brokerage's default 12-step plan. Adding steps is a Sunday decision based on data. Any sequence activation is "needs Caleb's approval before running."
- Follow-up pings should sound like Caleb texting a neighbor, with one new true detail if available (a nearby sale, an open house date).
- Every reply, including "not interested" or "remove me," stops the sequence and is honored immediately (see ce-compliant-outbound-guardrails).
- Track spam complaints and texting opt-outs per sequence in the CRM so AIRE can flag when a sequence is hurting the domain or number.

## Verification

- New sequences contain exactly two touches with different subjects.
- Follow-up is under 60 words and contains a one-line TLDR of the offer.
- A documented reply-rate threshold exists for adding a third step, and complaint rate is tracked alongside it.
