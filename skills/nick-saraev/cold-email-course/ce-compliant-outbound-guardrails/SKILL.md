# Compliant Outbound Guardrails (the lawful reading of "gray hat")

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapter 28, second half (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=12785s)

## What Nick teaches

Nick defines gray hat techniques as those that "trade compliance," meaning compliance with local laws and with platform terms of service (Gmail, Outlook, LinkedIn, and others). He says explicitly: he cannot and will not recommend them, Maker School members have had accounts permanently suspended and received warning letters, and any platform or jurisdiction can change the rules at any time. He covers them only so the course is complete and so you can recognize the downside. He names three:

1. **Buying or renting accounts and mailboxes.** Pre-warmed LinkedIn, Instagram, X, and TikTok accounts bought from vendors to get higher connect limits (LinkedIn roughly 100 to 200 connects per week, more on Premium or aged accounts). "Pre-warmed mailboxes" sold through platforms like Instantly or Smartlead with a first name, last name, and domain that may not belong to a real person, bought 50 to 100 at a time to skip the roughly 21-day warm-up. Some companies hire low-cost overseas staff and effectively rent their LinkedIn accounts under threat of losing the job. Nick calls that slimy and does not do it. Sending as "Stacy" who does not exist requires explaining it away later, either honestly or deceptively.
2. **Power dialers and auto voicemail drops.** Parallel dialing lifts a cold caller from about 20% to 50% time with real people (100 minutes of calling normally splits 20 dialing, 20 connecting, 20 gatekeeper, 20 voicemail, 20 decision maker). Automatic voicemail drops to 50,000 numbers without placing a call are regulated against.
3. **Cold SMS, cold WhatsApp, and iMessage emulation.** Platforms that blast texts across many numbers in parallel, or fake the blue iMessage bubble. Nick says cold messaging is "extremely regulated and very very no" and he "probably would not do this."

His closing: use according to your own risk tolerance, and understand he is not encouraging any of it.

**The lawful version of each lever** (what Caleb should actually do):
- Instead of bought personas: send as Caleb Jackson from a domain he owns, warm new mailboxes the slow way, and add sending capacity with additional real, disclosed mailboxes (caleb@, hello@) on his own domains.
- Instead of rented LinkedIn accounts: one real profile, Premium if useful, connect requests within LinkedIn's limits, and build the "reach" through content and referrals.
- Instead of cold SMS blasts: text only where consent or an established business relationship exists, respect opt-outs instantly, and use email or mail for true cold first touch.
- Instead of voicemail drops: live calls that are DNC-scrubbed, or voicemail left by a human after a real call attempt.
- Instead of tracking-pixel footers and hidden tools: clean plain-text sends, and the required identification and opt-out mechanics on commercial email.

## Step by step (pre-flight for every campaign)

1. **Channel check.** Email: commercial email to US recipients falls under CAN-SPAM. Include a truthful From and subject, a valid physical postal address, and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days. Nick skips opt-outs for B2B contacts from LinkedIn, but Caleb is a licensee marketing to consumers, so include one. Texts and calls: TCPA and the National Do Not Call Registry apply. Autodialed or prerecorded texts and calls to cell phones need prior express written consent. Scrub against the DNC list and Louisiana's state list, and keep an internal do-not-contact list. Ringless voicemail is treated as a call by the FCC. Verify current rules, they change.
2. **Identity check.** Every message is from Caleb by name, with his brokerage (Keller Williams First Choice) and license status identifiable in the message or signature, as Louisiana Real Estate Commission advertising rules require. No fake senders, no "my assistant Stacy" who does not exist, no pretending to be a buyer or a neighbor.
3. **Claims check.** No promises of a sale price, sale date, or guaranteed outcome. Offers are deliverables (reports, reviews, walkthroughs). Social proof uses real transactions and numbers Caleb can document. Testimonials only with permission.
4. **List check.** Where did the data come from and what are its use terms (MLS rules on expired and withdrawn listings, tax-roll data, purchased lists)? Expired listings: confirm the listing is actually off market and the owner is not under a current listing agreement with another broker before any solicitation.
5. **Fair housing check.** Segmenting by neighborhood, price, or property type is fine. Nothing about who lives there or protected characteristics, in targeting or in copy.
6. **Platform check.** Stay inside Gmail, Outlook, LinkedIn, Instagram, and X terms of service. No automation tools that violate them.
7. **Record keeping.** Log consent source, opt-outs, DNC scrub date, and the approved copy version per campaign.

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Permanent account suspensions and warning letters from trying bought accounts or blasting tools.
- Having to explain a fake sender on a sales call.
- Renting employees' social accounts under duress.
- Voicemail drops without a call.
- Any cold SMS or WhatsApp campaign at scale.
- Assuming rules are static. Platforms and governments change them at any time.

## How Caleb should use this

- Treat this as the gate before every other skill in this course. Nothing goes out without the pre-flight. Every send, schedule, or automation is "needs Caleb's approval before running."
- Build the suppression list as a first-class AIRE object: DNC scrubs, opt-outs, past clients who asked for space, and anyone represented by another broker.
- Mail (letters and postcards) is the safest cold first touch for homeowners, then email with proper footer, then consented text, then live call. Nick's copy principles apply to all of them.
- Have a Louisiana real estate attorney or the KW compliance contact review the footer, disclosures, and any guarantee-style offer once, then reuse.
- When Caleb hears a "gray hat" tactic from another agent, use this skill to name which lever it is and what the lawful substitute looks like.

## Verification

- Opt-outs and "stop" replies are honored and reflected in the suppression list within one business day.
- No message in the campaign uses a sender identity other than Caleb or a real, disclosed team member.
