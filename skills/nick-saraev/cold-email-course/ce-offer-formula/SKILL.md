# The Offer Formula

**Source:** Nick Saraev, "COLD EMAIL COPYWRITING & OUTREACH Full Course 2026", chapters 7, 8, 10, 11, 12 and 13 (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uSTGNHGFOAo&t=3058s)

## What Nick teaches

**The formula.** Conversion rate = (Return on investment you can convince them of) x (Trust that you will deliver it) / (Friction to get started). Nick does not calculate it. He uses it to keep attention on the three prime movers. Everything else "rounds to zero."

**The offer sentence.** "I will do X thing in Y time or Z risk mitigation."
- X must be **quantified** (a number) and **unambiguous** (define the thing: a "meeting" is a video call with a prospect, a "conversation" could be anything).
- Y must be **time bound**. Without a definition of done, the guarantee is meaningless ("one meeting this year, 19 in forty years").
- Z puts the risk on you: or you don't pay, or I work free until I do, or you get all your money back, or I send you a $500 gift card.
- No ranges. "$20K in 90 days," not "$10 to 20K in 2 or 3 months."

**Why guarantees work.** You are asking a stranger for a high-ticket decision. The only thing that cuts through the noise is an offer that sounds almost too good to be true, which earns the call where you explain that it is grounded. Offers roughly 3x top of funnel at a cost of around 10% margin on the ones you cannot fulfill, so net about 2.7x.

**Scale the promise to their business.** $100K in 60 days to a creator making $5 to 10M a year is a 2% improvement. $100K to a $300K/month business is 16%. The promise should sound huge to them and be easy for you. He also scales by revenue signal: if they make $100K he offers $20K, if $200K he offers $40K, trusting he can lift revenue 20%.

**Trust is built around the offer**, not inside it: rapport, social proof, in-group, authority (see ce-seven-trust-principles). Dental example: they promise 10 new patients in 30 days while mentioning they did 109 last week, so the promise looks trivially safe.

**Friction is minimized explicitly:** "I'll just need 15 minutes of your time over a brief call once at the beginning. We won't have to talk again until I deliver all 20 meetings." Then a default action path: "How does 3:30pm today sound? I can ring you at <number> or send a one-click Google Meet invite."

**Nick's offer bank** (outcome / time / risk reversal):
- Lead gen: "I'll guarantee you 20 booked sales appointments in the next 60 days or you don't pay, no strings. Just say yes and I'll get started." (He notes "just say yes" campaigns feel tired now.)
- Live chat SaaS: "I'll build a world-class live chat widget on your website at no cost. I won't charge you anything until you get your first 10 paying clients." (No time needed because it is self-liquidating.)
- Proposal system: "I'll build you the same high-converting proposal template that's made <recognizable company> $5M in the last 2 months. I'll do all the work up front. Only if you like it will I ask you to work with me. Just say yes and I'll send yours within 48 hours."
- SEO: "I'll run a full SEO audit on your site and show you exactly which pages are losing you traffic, completely free. Just say yes and I'll have it in your inbox within 24 hours."
- Google Ads: "I'll build you a Google Ads campaign that I guarantee will generate 50 qualified leads in 30 days or you don't pay a cent. Just say yes and I'll get it live this week."
- Content: "Just send us a title and we'll give you a completely free 500-word blog post on the topic, no strings." (Scaled 1SecondCopy to about $92K/month.)
- CRM: "I'll build you a world-class industry CRM at no cost, pay for it all myself, do all the work up front, and only if you like it will I ask you to work with me. Link within 48 hours."
- Dental CME: "Completely free entry into <program> which lets you fulfill your 20 CME credit requirement at no cost. Just say yes and we'll send a private invite link."
- Email marketing: "Send me your last three email campaigns. I'll rewrite them for free and show you exactly why the new versions convert better. No strings."
- Web design: "I'll redesign your homepage for free, mockup within 72 hours, you only pay if you absolutely love it" or "only pay if you get 5% or more higher CVR."

**System over template.** Templates decay as the market gets used to them. The system (this formula) produces endless templates. Strategy beats tactics.

## Step by step

1. Name the real deliverable in plain words (a number, a document, a meeting, a redesign). Define it so there is no ambiguity.
2. Attach a number and a deadline. Pick a number that is small for you and big for them.
3. Choose the risk reversal: free up front, pay only if you like it, pay only on result, money back, or work until done.
4. Write the friction line: how little they must do, how many times you will talk.
5. Add the default action path with a time and a one-click way to start.
6. Read it against ROI x Trust / Friction and ask which of the three is weakest. Strengthen that one.
7. Check the promise is something you can lawfully and honestly make (see ce-compliant-outbound-guardrails).

## Templates / prompts

```
I'll {{deliverable with number}} in {{time}} or {{risk reversal}}.
It'll take {{tiny ask}} on your end and we won't need to talk again until {{done condition}}.
How does {{time 1}} sound? I can ring you at {{phone}} or send a one-click invite.
```

Real estate offers (lawful, deliverable-based, not price promises):
```
Seller: "I'll hand you a written value range for your place plus the three things I'd fix before listing, in 48 hours, at no cost and no listing commitment. All I need is a 10-minute walk-through."
Expired: "I'll show you exactly why the last listing stalled (photos, price band, days on market vs the 6 comps that did sell) in a one-page review by Thursday. Free, and you keep it either way."
Absentee owner: "I'll run a rent-vs-sell number on the property in 24 hours, no cost, so you can see what holding vs selling looks like this year. Just reply 'yes'."
Investor: "I'll send you every off-market and pre-list opportunity I see in West Feliciana for the next 30 days, first, before it hits anyone else. No fee, you only work with me if one is worth it."
```

## Pitfalls Nick calls out

- Ambiguous deliverables ("conversations," "support," "help you grow").
- Missing timeframes.
- Ranges and hedges.
- Making the start hard ("free, but it requires a 10-hour workshop").
- Fully financial offers in most niches. He prefers deliverable-based ones and only used revenue guarantees to illustrate.
- Putting pricing in the email.

- Caleb's version of Z is usually "free, no listing commitment, you keep the report either way," not a price or timing guarantee. Louisiana licensing rules and the fiduciary context mean promises of a sale price or sale date are off the table.

- For AIRE: store each segment's offer sentence as a reusable block and A/B two risk-reversal phrasings per the ce-sunday-iteration skill.

## Verification

- Offer sentence contains a number, a deadline, and a risk reversal, with no range.
- A stranger could restate what they get and when in one breath.
- Friction is stated in minutes and number of conversations.
