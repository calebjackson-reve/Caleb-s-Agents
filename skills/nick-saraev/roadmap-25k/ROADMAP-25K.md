# Nick Saraev's "Roadmap to $25K/Month With Automation"

Source: https://whimsical.com/roadmap-to-25k-month-with-automation-P5K6f1h9YpY5ngzvCW93Pg
Read on 2026-10-15 by rendering the board at 97% zoom and transcribing every node. Links on the board (Niche Discovery sheet, Upwork roasts, Sales System video, ZenPilot template, etc.) are Nick's own resources and are not reproduced here.

## What it is

Two things on one board.

**Left side: the automation customer journey.** A flowchart of how a client moves through an automation business, in three stages.

1. **Marketing and lead generation.** Six channels feed one funnel: Referrals, Communities, Cold Email, Platform Sales (Upwork), LinkedIn Outreach, Inbound Social. Nick's note: if you have no following, outbound is the simplest and most reliable. Inbound works once you have brand equity.
2. **Sales.** Meeting booked. No show, then follow-up cycle and re-book. Show, then one sales meeting that is both discovery and close. Not a fit right now, then follow-up cycle, wait 30 days, retry. Not a fit ever, then opt out. Move forward, then send a detailed proposal and statement of work. Not signed, then follow-up cycle and re-send. Signed and paid, then fulfillment. Nick prefers this one-call model because documents and emails are easy to automate and it keeps the hoops low.
3. **Project management and fulfillment.** Kickoff call. Create entry in the agency project manager. Fulfill project. Record a delivery video and send a templated message. Revisions loop back. No revisions, mark completed. Payment in tranches, collect delivery payment. Then the money step most people skip: send a templated message requesting additional work. Client accepts, loop back to kickoff. Client does not accept, add to follow-up queue and reach out in 30 days. His point: the client never sees inside the black box. They only see deliverables. Dress up the outside, and the period right after delivery is your highest ROI sales time. Recurring revenue is priority one.

**Right side: the 110-step roadmap.** 22 purple "main" steps in order, each with blue sub-steps. His claim: follow it start to finish, assuming no network, no referrals and no skills beyond automation, and you end up with a business doing $25K a month. Steps are ordered so that daily sales activity starts as early as possible.

## The 22 steps, with Nick's sub-steps

| # | Step | Sub-steps as written on the board |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Define your niche using the Niche Discovery sheet | "Everything for everyone" isn't scalable. First define a specific market segment (designers? coaches?). Then identify system type (sales? project management?). End result: "I build X systems for Y niche." |
| 2 | Come up with a business name using Namelix or similar | Names don't matter. 5 minute timer, short and memorable. Verify the .com is free. |
| 3 | Build 5 case studies or portfolio pieces for niche plus templates | Use systems you've already built as inspiration, or Nick's videos and blueprints. Build Make.com blueprints and template each one. Templates get you 80% of the way, your job is the other 20% (customizing). Massive leverage. |
| 4 | Buy main domain plus 3 sending domains | .com main domain. Sending domains are variations: get-, start-, kit-. Namecheap or similar. |
| 5 | Create and publish website with a drag-and-drop builder | Webflow or whatever you know. Don't build from scratch, buy a template and tweak. The site is a business card for legitimacy. Under 3 hours. |
| 6 | Get Google Workspace Starter on main domain | Add primary domain, create name@ address. Downgrade to Business Starter ($6 to 7 instead of $15, as stated then). |
| 7 | Add three sending domains as secondaries, 3 mailboxes each | 9 mailboxes total. first@, first.l@, first.last@. Profile pic on each (important). Add SPF and DMARC. Calendar reminder for 48 hours, then add DKIM. |
| 8 | Get Instantly or Smartlead and start warming mailboxes | Connect all domains. Turn on warmup, calendar reminder for 20 days. Source first batch of leads (LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo). Enrich emails (Anymailfinder, DropContact). Add personalization with an LLM. Set up response templates and macros. After 20 days, launch at 30 emails per mailbox x 9 = 270 a day. |
| 9 | Create Upwork profile using best practices | Watch Upwork roasts. Polished photo on a bright background. Headline optimized for keywords. Above the fold and below the fold copy. Non-round pricing. Fill every field. Get first 2 reviews from legitimate clients or people in your network. |
| 10 | Find 3 communities and set up a content calendar | Google, Skool, Discord, Slack, Facebook, Telegram. Join many, keep three that are active and not spam pits. Read top posts, find member problem areas. 3x a week post a thread solving those problems. All value, no public pitching. When someone comments, DM to see if they need more help. Offer to book calls and show them how to do it themselves. |
| 11 | Set up CRM on ClickUp, Monday or alternative | Keep it basic. Flesh it out after you have customers. Customers over admin. |
| 12 | Create proposal template on PandaDoc or alternative | Start from a template. Combine agreement and proposal so it's one step. Payment integration so they get an invoice at signing. |
| 13 | Set up Stripe or alternative | Learn invoices vs payments, subscriptions. |
| 14 | Set daily calendar reminders to tackle sales systematically | At 0 customers your only priority is sales. Misprioritization is why 90%+ fail. Every morning apply to at least 5 Upwork jobs. Morning, afternoon and evening run through the cold email inbox and community responses. Under 2 hours a day, the highest leverage 2 hours you'll have. Watch the Sales System video first, then set up Cal.com or Calendly. |
| 15 | After first customer won, time project completion | Track every minute (Harvest, Toggl). What you don't track won't improve. Notepad is fine. To manage: kickoff call, fulfillment, video deliverable, revisions. |
| 16 | Calculate hourly rate and do a retrospective | How long did it take, how much did you pitch? Be honest about $/hr. Two questions: what went well, what could I have improved? Your job is now to raise $/hr on every project and rinse and repeat. |
| 17 | Set up project management on ClickUp, Monday or alternative | Not before project 1 because it detracts from sales. ZenPilot template or similar. Needed to scale a team, less so freelancing. |
| 18 | Scale customer acquisition until $10K revenue | Rinse and repeat. Daily sales actions and repeated retrospectives make this straightforward. After a dozen projects your rate can exceed $200/hr, and at $200/hr, $10K a month is 10 to 15 hours a week. |
| 19 | Optimize sales funnel by ROI | $0 to $10K needs agility and consistency. $10K to $25K needs systems. Look at every past sale: where did it come from? Break down by source and internal $/hr. Double down and 10x what works: 10x the mailboxes, 10x the communities, 10x the Upwork apps. Then build infrastructure to manage it: auto-draft email responses, auto-generate community guides, hire someone to send Upwork apps so you only record. |
| 20 | Optimize project management by ROI | Same principle. For every success or failure, what's the root cause? Find bottlenecks in PM and build systems. |
| 21 | Scale customer acquisition until $25K revenue | Possible alone but harder. Solo at $25K is probably 90%+ margin. Growing an agency is often less profitable than doing it yourself. Past $25K solo you need network effects (social) or non-service offers (communities, courses), which is what Nick does. |
| 22 | Want to scale a team? | Sit tight for his Scaling Past $25K Roadmap. |
