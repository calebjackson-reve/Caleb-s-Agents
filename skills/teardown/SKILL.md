# Teardown

Caleb is a REALTOR with Keller Williams First Choice and the builder of AIRE. This skill runs the reverse engineering loop from the "Reverse Engineer Everything" lesson (`lessons/reverse-engineer-everything/LESSON.md`): take it apart, get blueprint, proof and unknowns, rebuild, compare.

## 1. Intake

Confirm these before starting. Ask only for what is missing.

- **The asset:** link, transcript, screenshots, file or a description of it
- **Why it was picked:** what result it got (saves, DMs, leads, a listing won)
- **The rebuild target:** what Caleb wants from it (a video for a specific listing, a neighborhood post, a page, a follow-up sequence, an internal tool)

If you can't open a link (some hosts are blocked in cloud sessions), say so and ask for a transcript or screenshots. Never invent what the asset contains.

## 2. Take it apart

Use the checklist for the asset type.

**Short video or reel**
- Hook in the first 3 seconds: visual, spoken words, on-screen text
- The promise that keeps a viewer watching
- Beats with timestamps
- Shot types and pacing (cuts per 10 seconds)
- On-screen text and caption style
- Audio: voice, music, sound effects
- Length and loop point
- Call to action and where it sends people
- Comments: questions, objections, arguments

**Listing presentation**
- Order of sections and why that order works
- Where proof shows up (stats, past results, reviews) and how it is sourced
- How pricing is introduced
- How objections are handled before they come up
- The ask and the next step

**Landing page or funnel** (public pages only, as a visitor)
- Headline and offer
- Steps from first click to contact, counted
- What is asked for at each step
- Trust signals
- Follow-up promised

**Email or text sequence**
- Timing between touches
- Subject line or opener pattern
- One job per message
- Calls to action and how they escalate

**Software feature**
- What it visibly does, step by step, from the user's side
- Inputs, outputs and edge cases
- The smallest version Caleb would actually use

## 3. Report

Always use exactly these three headings.

### Blueprint
How it works, step by step, in plain words. This is the "pseudocode."

### Proof
The evidence for each blueprint step: timestamps, quotes, screenshots, metrics, comment patterns. A step with no proof gets moved to Unknowns.

### Unknowns
What can't be told from the material provided, for example paid boost spend, audience size, or what happened off camera. Name the gap. Don't guess.

## 4. Rebuild

Write **two original versions, A and B**, for Caleb's target. They share the blueprint and differ in one variable, usually the hook.

- Copy the mechanics, not the content. No reused footage, music, script wording, thumbnails, listing media or branding from the source.
- Ground it in Caleb's real material: his listings, his market, the questions he hears every week. If you don't have that material, ask for it or leave a clearly marked placeholder.
- Voice: warm, direct and natural. No em dashes, no semicolons, no generic AI phrasing, no corporate polish.
- Branding: Keller Williams First Choice for current work. Never use Rêve branding, fonts, colors or brokerage language unless Caleb explicitly asks.
- Market stats show their source and date. Unsourced numbers don't go in.
- Keep Fair Housing in mind in both content and suggested targeting.

For video, deliver per version: hook (spoken plus on-screen text), beat-by-beat script with rough timings, shot list, caption and call to action.

## 5. Measure

End the report with a short test plan:

- Which version to post first and when the other runs
- The value metrics to judge it on: saves, shares, DMs, link clicks, booked calls. Views and likes are tracked but don't decide.
- When to check (7 days after posting is the default)

## Guardrails

- Never post, send, schedule, publish or spend money. Drafts only. Caleb approves every outward action.
- Don't decompile licensed software to clone or resell it. Rebuild from visible behavior, for internal use. Flag anything unclear for an attorney.
- Don't submit fake leads, showing requests or form entries on anyone's site.
- Label what came from the source, what is your inference, and what is unverified.
