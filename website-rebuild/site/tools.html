Taxes

Write-offs to ask your CPA about

Federal rules for tax year 2026, after the 2025 tax law changes. Every item links to its source. Bring this list to your CPA, not your tax return.

Homeowners

  • Mortgage interest

    Interest on up to $750,000 of loan used to buy or build your main home ($375,000 married filing separately). The 2025 tax law made that cap permanent.

    Source: IRS Publication 936

  • Points

    Points paid to buy your main home are usually deductible the year you pay them. Points on a refinance are spread over the loan.

    Source: IRS Topic 504

  • Property taxes

    State and local taxes, including property tax, are deductible up to a cap of $40,000 for 2025, rising 1% a year through 2029. Higher incomes get a smaller cap.

    Source: IRS, Working Families Tax Cuts

  • Mortgage insurance

    Mortgage insurance premiums are deductible again starting in 2026. Ask your CPA about the income limits.

    Source: NAHB tax reform summary

  • Home office

    Only if you're self-employed or own a business and use the space regularly and only for that business. W-2 employees can't take it.

    Source: IRS Publication 587

  • Selling your home

    Up to $250,000 of gain is tax free ($500,000 married filing jointly) if you owned and lived there 2 of the last 5 years. Commissions, title and closing costs you pay reduce the gain.

    Source: IRS Publication 523

  • Improvements raise your basis

    A new roof, HVAC, an addition. Keep every receipt, because they lower the taxable gain when you sell. Repairs don't count.

    Source: IRS Publication 523

  • Energy credits ended

    The energy efficient home improvement and residential solar credits stopped after December 31, 2025. Don't count on them for a 2026 project.

    Source: IRS, Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit

  • Louisiana homestead exemption

    The first $75,000 of your home's market value is exempt from parish property tax if it's your primary residence. File with your parish assessor.

    Source: Louisiana Assessors' Association

Rental and commercial investors

  • Depreciation

    Rental houses are depreciated over 27.5 years, commercial buildings over 39. Land never depreciates.

    Source: IRS Publication 527

  • Operating expenses

    Insurance, management fees, mortgage interest, property taxes, utilities, advertising, legal and accounting, and travel to the property.

    Source: IRS Publication 527

  • Repairs vs. improvements

    Repairs are deducted the same year. Improvements get depreciated. Items up to $2,500 each can often be expensed under the de minimis safe harbor.

    Source: IRS, Tangible Property Regulations

  • Bonus depreciation and cost segregation

    100% bonus depreciation is back for qualifying property bought after January 19, 2025. A cost segregation study moves parts of a building into faster schedules so more of it qualifies.

    Source: IRS, Cost Segregation Guide

  • 1031 exchange

    Sell investment or business property and buy like-kind real estate to defer the gain. 45 days to name the replacement, 180 days to close, and a qualified intermediary holds the money. Your own home doesn't qualify.

    Source: IRS, Like-Kind Exchanges

  • $25,000 rental loss allowance

    If you actively manage your rental, up to $25,000 of losses can offset other income. It phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of modified AGI.

    Source: IRS Publication 925

  • 20% business income deduction

    The qualified business income deduction is now permanent. A rental has to run like a business to qualify, and there's a 250-hour safe harbor.

    Source: IRS, QBI Deduction

General information only, not tax or legal advice. Tax rules change and every situation is different, so talk with a licensed CPA or tax professional before acting. Checked against the linked sources October 20, 2026.

Selling

Ways to add value before you sell

  1. Fix what an inspector will flag

    Roof, HVAC, water heater, leaks, electrical panel. Buyers negotiate hardest on the inspection report, so handle the big items first.

  2. Paint, light and curb appeal

    Neutral paint, bright bulbs, a clean yard and a front door that looks cared for. Small money, first impression.

  3. Get a pre-listing inspection

    Find the surprises before a buyer does, then fix them or price them in.

  4. Keep a receipts folder

    Every improvement with a date and a cost. It supports your price and lowers your taxable gain later.

  5. Check your homestead and tax bill

    Make sure the exemption is filed and the assessment is right. It matters to your budget and to the next buyer.

  6. Flood zone and elevation certificate

    In Louisiana buyers ask about flood insurance early. Having the elevation certificate ready keeps a deal moving.

  7. Price it on real comps

    The best improvement is the right list price. Ask me for a free CMA before you spend money on projects.
