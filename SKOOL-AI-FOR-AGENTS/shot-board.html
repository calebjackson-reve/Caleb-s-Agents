The set

One spot. Never change it.

Every video and every clip comes from this one chair. Same shirt on batch day. Same light every Tuesday after that.

  1. Camera: iPhone rear camera, 4K at 30fps, on a tripod at eye level, 5 to 6 feet from you. Tap and hold on your face to lock focus and exposure.
  2. Mic: clip-on wireless mic. Bad audio is the only thing that makes people leave. Bad video they forgive.
  3. Light: a window to one side at about 45 degrees, or one soft light there. Never a window behind you.
  4. Background: a real room with depth. A shelf, a lamp, a plant. Nothing you'd have to tidy every time. No KW signage until the brokerage says yes.
  5. Screen: record the Mac with Shift, Command and 5. Browser at 125%. Every tab, email and notification closed. No client info on screen, ever.
  6. Framing: head and shoulders, eyes on the top third line, a little room above your head. This is the "wide" in every storyboard below.
Buy nothing without saying yes first. If you don't own a clip-on mic, the iPhone wired earbuds mic beats the built-in mic for a test shoot.
The formula

How Nate Herk and Nick Saraev make a video

I could not watch their footage from this server, so this is built from their video posts, their group pages and what their format is known for. Treat it as a strong read, not a frame-by-frame study. Open the references and check it against your own eyes before batch day.

Face, then screen by 0:20

They open on their face with the promise, then cut to the screen before the viewer can wonder what this is about.

Build it live

You watch them type the prompt, see the result and react. That reaction is the content. Your version: type, read, say "this is what I'd send."

Zoom on the thing

They punch in on the exact prompt box or button. A full browser is unreadable on a phone. Scale 150 to 200%.

One promise per title

"Follow-up texts that get answered," not "AI tips." The title is delivered in the first 30 seconds.

Picture changes every 5 to 10 seconds

Punch-in, screen cut, B-roll or text card. Nothing sits still long enough to scroll away.

Batch day

The 4-hour filming order

On-camera parts first while your energy is high. Screen recordings second, they can be redone any time.

TimeWhatNotes
0:00 to 0:30Setup and a 60-second testWatch the test on your phone with the sound up. Fix light and audio now, not later.
0:30 to 2:00On-camera lines for all 8 videos, Day 0 to Day 7Read one line off the phone, look up, say it. Three takes max per line. Keep the last good one.
2:00 to 2:15BreakWater, not coffee. Your voice has 2 more hours.
2:15 to 3:45Screen recordings, Day 1 to Day 7Fresh chat for each day. Type the prompts for real so the results are real. Speed up in the edit.
