# Lesson 2: Talk to It Like a New Assistant

Module 1: The Basics · About 9 minutes

## Teaching plan

- **Goal:** Teach the single habit that makes AI output good: give it context.
- **Psychology idea:** The curse of knowledge. When you know something well, you forget what others don't know. Caleb's story of losing the group.
- **The one win:** Rewrite a listing description with a context-rich prompt.
- **Screen demo:** A weak prompt ("write a listing description") vs a strong prompt with who, what, who it's for, tone and length.
- **Template to show on screen:** Who you are. What you need. Who it's for. The details. How it should sound. How long.

## Script

**[On camera]**
I once tried to teach a room full of agents how I use AI. Five minutes in, I'd lost every one of them.

I jumped straight to the advanced stuff. I forgot what it was like to not know it. There's a name for that. It's called the curse of knowledge. When you know something well, you forget what the other person doesn't know.

Here's the funny part. Most people make the exact same mistake with AI. Just in reverse.

**[On camera]**
You know your listing. You know the neighborhood, the buyer, the vibe. AI knows none of it. So when you type "write a listing description," it guesses. And guesses sound generic.

The fix is simple. Talk to it like a brand new assistant on their first day. Tell it everything.

**[Screen]**
Watch. Weak version first:
"Write a listing description."

Enter. See how it's full of "stunning" and "nestled" and made-up details? That's AI guessing.

Now the strong version. I use six pieces. Who I am. What I need. Who it's for. The details. How it should sound. How long.

"I'm a real estate agent in Zachary, Louisiana. Write a listing description for a 3 bed, 2.5 bath, 2,043 square foot home on a golf course with a private courtyard and a whole-home generator. It's for buyers moving up from their first home. Make it warm and simple, no fancy words like stunning or nestled. Keep it under 120 words."

Enter.

**[Screen, reading]**
Night and day. It sounds like a person. It uses my real details.

One more trick. If something's off, just tell it. "Make it shorter." "Mention the generator first." It's a conversation, not a vending machine.

**[On camera]**
Always check the facts before you post anything. AI can still get details wrong. You're the agent. It's the assistant.

Your one action: take one of your listings, or a past one, and write the six-piece prompt. Post the before and after in the Skool group. The six-piece template is pinned in the Classroom.
