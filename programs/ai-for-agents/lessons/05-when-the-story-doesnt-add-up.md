# Lesson 5: When the Story Doesn't Add Up

Module 2: Talking to Clients · About 9 minutes

## Teaching plan

- **Goal:** Teach agents to notice mismatches and ask clarifying questions, instead of trying to "read lies."
- **Psychology idea:** Research shows body-language lie detection is weak. What works is noticing when words, actions and timelines don't match, then asking calm follow-ups.
- **The one win:** A personal list of clarifying questions, built with AI, for buyers and sellers.
- **Screen demo:** Give AI a made-up scenario with mismatches (says urgent, won't schedule showings). Ask it to list the mismatches and suggest non-confrontational questions.

## Script

**[On camera]**
Every agent has had a client who said one thing and did another.

"We're ready to buy now." Then they cancel every showing. "Price it to sell." Then they reject every reasonable offer.

You might be wondering how to tell when someone isn't telling you the truth. Here's what surprised me. Most of what we've heard about spotting lies, like looking away or crossing arms, doesn't hold up well in research. People are pretty bad at reading lies from body language.

What does work is simpler. Notice when the story doesn't match. Then ask about it, calmly.

**[On camera]**
Three things to compare. What they say. What they do. And the timeline. When those line up, you're probably getting the truth. When they don't, there's something you don't know yet.

And usually it's not a lie. It's fear, a spouse who isn't on board, or money they're embarrassed about.

**[Screen]**
Let's use AI to sharpen this. I'll describe a made-up client.

"Here's a buyer situation. They say they need to move within 30 days. They've been pre-approved for 3 months but haven't made an offer. They cancel showings last minute and only one spouse ever comes. List the mismatches you see. Then give me 5 calm, non-pushy questions I could ask to understand what's really going on."

**[Screen, reading]**
Look. It caught that only one spouse shows up. And it suggests: "How does your partner feel about the homes we've seen so far?" That's gentle. It's not an accusation.

**[On camera]**
The goal is never to catch someone. It's to understand them so you can actually help. When people feel understood, the truth tends to come out on its own.

Your one action: ask AI to build you a list of 10 clarifying questions for buyers and 10 for sellers. Save it in your phone. Post your favorite in the group.
