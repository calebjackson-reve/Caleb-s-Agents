# Caleb's lingo

The voice file every AI for Agents script is written against. Claude reads this before drafting a hook, a script or on-screen text. Caleb approves every script before he films it.

Where this came from:
- **Source: his take.** The iMessage lesson he filmed on 2026-10-14 (IMG_6269.MOV, 115 s), transcribed with whisper on 2026-10-15.
- **Source: his messages.** His own messages in the project, 2026-10-13 to 2026-10-15.
- **Inference.** Claude's read of the patterns. Change these freely.

## How he opens

- **Source: his take.** "What's going on guys? So I just want to introduce myself, I'm Caleb Jackson. I am a realtor with Keller Williams First Choice. I've been in this industry for about four and a half years."
- **Source: his messages.** He wants the intro said his way: "Hey, I'm Caleb Jackson…"
- **Rule (decided 2026-10-13).** 80% of the planning goes into the first 3 seconds. Plan the hook, the first words, the shot and the angle. Give a "me too" line, not a "so what" line. Make it about the viewer, never about him.
- **Practice (inference).** His natural intro is about him, so it goes second. Open on the viewer's problem, then "I'm Caleb Jackson" over a title card. The pilot does this: it opens on "Your next real estate conversation may already be in your iMessages, and we just don't know it yet."

## How he explains

- **Real people as examples (source: his take).** He uses short names, a role and one detail each. "Taylor is a friend asking about dinner. Alex is a plumber talking about repairs at a listing."
- **Quoting the client (source: his take).** He reads the client's actual words out loud. "We're thinking about buying or selling next spring. Could you help us understand what we need to do first?"
- **Safety line (source: his take).** He says out loud that a human checks the work. "You review it before anything goes out." "This is just a prototype with fictional data."
- **The promise (source: his take).** He ends on what the viewer walks away with. "By the end of these lessons, you'll have… a clear follow-up list built from what people actually said."
- **Talking over reading (source: his messages and the 2026-10-14 take).** He talks better than he reads. He ignored the written script and that take was the one used. So scripts give him the hook word for word and loose beats for the rest.

## Pace

- **Source: his take, measured.** 328 words from first word to last word in 112 s is about 176 words a minute. Nick Saraev runs about 235. The editor trims pauses, but the words stay his speed. Animation carries the energy so he doesn't have to rush.

## Write it this way

- Short sentences. One idea each.
- "You" and "your" in the first line.
- Plain real estate words: lead, listing, follow-up, client, vendor, closing.
- On screen, use 3 to 7 words per headline, with one accent word.
- Keller Williams First Choice appears as text only, as policy allows.

## Never

- No em dashes or semicolons.
- No generic AI phrasing: "unlock", "leverage", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world", "dive in", "supercharge", "seamless".
- No corporate polish, and no "about me" opener.
- No Rêve branding, fonts, colors or brokerage language.
- No market number without a source and date.
- No real client names or client financial data. Use fictional people, flagged on screen.
- Fair Housing: never sort, score or describe people by a protected class. Motivation questions ask about timing and plans only.
