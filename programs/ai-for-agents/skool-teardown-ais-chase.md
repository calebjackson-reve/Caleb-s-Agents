# Teardown: AI Automation Society and Chase AI

Pulled live from Skool on 2026-10-12. The goal is to see how these two grew so big and what they teach, then turn that into moves for AI for Agents.

**What I could and couldn't see**
- AI Automation Society is a public group, so I read its feed, its pinned posts, the descriptions of all 17 courses and its paid tier page.
- Chase AI Community is private. I could only read its about page and its paid tier page.
- I couldn't watch the videos. YouTube blocks this cloud server, so I worked from the creators' own written summaries in each video post. If you want a specific video broken down, paste its transcript and I'll do it like the Reverse Engineer lesson.

## How they grew (the pattern both use)

1. **YouTube is the engine. Skool is where people land.** Nate posts a "🚀New Video" in the group every 1 to 3 days (Oct 8, 9 and 11 this week). Every video gives away a template or file that lives in the free group. You join to get the download.
2. **Free resources as the bait.** Both lead with "100+ n8n templates," "Claude Code skills," "full GitHub repos." The free group has a course called "All YouTube Resources: everything shown on my YT."
3. **A challenge gives new members a path.** AIS runs a "7 Day AIS Challenge: 7 days, 7 builds." Members post "Day 1 build" and "Day 2 build" in the feed, which keeps it active and shows proof. Graduates get listed in their own course. That's a certificate and status for free.
4. **A two-tier model, explained honestly.** Nate's pinned post says the free group "can be noisy... there's no path through it... I rarely answer questions here." The paid tier is "the opposite": a roadmap, a smaller group, daily answers and a weekly Q&A. Free is for reach. Paid is for access to him.
5. **Courses unlock over time in the paid tier.** "Get your first clients" opens after 30 days and "Scale" after 90. That keeps people paying and in order.
6. **Engagement mechanics.**
   - A welcome post pinned since day 1: "Introduce yourself + share a career goal." It has 106,000 comments.
   - Rules ban self-promotion and "DM me" posts, so the feed stays useful.
   - 9 named levels (AI Explorer up to AI Grandmaster) and a leaderboard. 89% of members stay at Level 1, so the active 11% carry the feed.
   - Auto-DM to new members is turned on in both groups.
   - Join questions ask for email and "where did you hear about us?" That's list building plus tracking where members come from.
7. **Proof everywhere.** Success-story courses, a reviews link, and revenue in the pitch ("my $5M+ per year business," "agents I sell that net me $40k/m").
8. **Extra income layers.** Affiliate links (a speech-to-text tool, a discount codes course), merch and a podcast. Chase pays affiliates 40% for bringing in members.
9. **Urgency on paid.** Chase: "40% flash sale, ends 10/10" and "price increase at 1.2k members."

## What value they teach

- **The promise is money, not knowledge.** "Learn to get paid for AI solutions." "Go from learning to earning." "Learn AI. Land clients."
- **Built for beginners.** "Master AI. Zero code." "Built for people with zero technical background."
- **They stay current.** Both moved from n8n to Claude Code in 2026, and Nate archived his old n8n material. Video topics follow the news: new Claude repos, Anthropic's guidance, tool updates.
- **Build, then show.** Every lesson ends with something you can show: a portfolio piece, a working assistant, a certificate.

## What to take for AI for Agents

**Copy these as they are**
- **One lesson video a week, with a free download that only lives in Skool.** Ideas: the prompt from the lesson, a follow-up script or a listing video template. The video's call to action becomes "grab the template in my free Skool."
- **A 7-day challenge as the front door.** For example, "7 Days to Your First AI Assistant": one 10-minute task a day, posted in the feed. It reuses Lessons 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8 and 10.
- **The pinned welcome post asks for a goal.** "Where do you sell, and what's one thing you want AI to take off your plate?"
- **Strict rules** against recruiting or "DM me" posts. That matters even more with agents.
- **Named levels.** For example: New Agent, Prompt Starter, Follow-Up Pro, Content Machine, AI-Run Business.
- **Join questions that track the source.** "Where did you hear about us?" shows whether Facebook, YouTube or invites are working.

**Adapt for agents**
- **Their promise is "make money selling AI." Yours is "close more and think clearer with AI."** Keep it tied to the agent's own income and hours saved, not a new business.
- **Proof.** They post revenue. You post agent wins: "saved 4 hours on follow-ups," "this text got a reply from a 6-month-cold lead." Collect them in a Wins section from week 3.
- **Paid tier later**, built like theirs: free gets the lessons and the community, paid gets you. That means a weekly live Q&A, answers inside a day and an unlockable roadmap. Their prices ($99 to $129 a month) are for people trying to start an agency. Agent groups that stuck price at $27 to $49 (see `skool-research.md`).

**Skip these**
- Flash sales and "price going up" countdowns this early. They don't fit a group of 100 agents who know you personally.
- Showing your income. Showing your process fits your voice and the psychology angle better.
- Affiliate links until there's trust, and then only for tools you actually use.
