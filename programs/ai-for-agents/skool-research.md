# Skool Market Research: AI for Real Estate Agents

Pulled live from skool.com on 2026-10-12. I searched Skool Discovery for "real estate ai", "ai for realtors", "real estate agents", "realtor", "real estate coaching", "louisiana real estate" and "chatgpt real estate" and read 291 groups. Member counts and prices are what Skool showed that day.

## Two things to decide first

1. **skool.com/aire is taken.** A group called "AI for Real Estate | AIRE" (21 members, free, created June 2026) belongs to a DFW agent. It uses your AIRE name for the same topic. A Skool URL can't be shared, so you can't use /aire. Whether that matters for the AIRE brand is your call. It may be worth a trademark check.
2. **skool.com/ai-for-agents is taken** by "AI for Real Estate Agents" ($39/month, 7 members). The display name "AI for Agents with Caleb Jackson" can still be used. Pick a different URL, for example `skool.com/caleb-jackson` or `skool.com/ai-for-agents-caleb`. Check that it's free when you set the group up.

## What this tells us

**Confirmed from the data**
- The audience is real. Free agent groups reach 5,000 to 16,000 members.
- AI for agents is crowded at the bottom. Dozens of groups started in 2025 and 2026, and most have under 25 members.
- Free groups are much bigger than paid ones. Of the AI groups over 500 members, only one charges, and it charges once.
- Paid AI groups that stuck price from $27 to $127 a month and sit at 50 to 300 members.

**My inference**
- 100 members in 90 days is realistic. It would put you in the top 10 or so AI-for-agents groups on Skool.
- Stay free to start, as the plan says. Add a paid tier after 100 members, in the $27 to $49 range, or keep selling one-on-ones.
- Most of those groups sell the tools: prompts, automations, "save 10 hours." Your angle, AI plus the psychology of why clients decide, is open. The closest thing is Tri's Sales Dojo at 1,537 members, which is psychology without AI. Lead with that.
- Your edge over the 60 stalled groups is distribution. They're strangers posting to strangers. You have about 500 agent friends who already know you, plus a local Louisiana angle nobody owns.
- Copy what the big free groups do: one clear promise in the description, with a number in it where you can.
