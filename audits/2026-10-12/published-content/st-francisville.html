Start with the way you want to live

Begin with your daily routine. How often will you drive to Baton Rouge or Zachary? How much outdoor space will you use? Do you want a house you can move into or one you can make your own? Those answers help narrow the search before a pretty listing starts making the decisions for you.

If being near Ferdinand Street matters to you, drive the actual route from the property. If you want acreage outside town, spend time looking at the land as well as the house. Walk the usable areas, ask about maintenance and find out how the property is accessed. A mailing address alone does not establish town boundaries, utilities or which office handles permits.

For a Baton Rouge commute, test the drive at the times you actually travel. Add your usual stops and the return trip. A map estimate can be a starting point, but it cannot tell you whether that routine works for you. Compare the house and the week you will live there.

Town homes, older properties and acreage

A home in or near town

Check the exact lot, access, parking and utilities. Ask what conveys with the sale and whether there are recorded restrictions. If your plans include a fence, addition or separate building, confirm what approvals apply to that address before you make those plans part of your offer.

An older home or a renovation project

Look beyond cosmetic updates. Ask about the roof, electrical work, plumbing, foundation and past repairs. Review permits and have qualified professionals evaluate the work you want to do. The Town of St. Francisville publishes historic district guidelines and planning information. Confirm whether the property is covered and which approvals your project needs. A property's age and its historic designation are separate questions.

Land and homes with acreage

Ask for the survey, legal access information and any recorded easements or restrictions. Verify where you can build and how water, electricity and sewer service reach the property. If it has a private well or an individual wastewater system, ask for the system records and arrange the appropriate checks. Louisiana's Onsite Wastewater Program and private-well real estate testing guidance are useful starting points. Confirm the lender's requirements early.

Build the ownership budget before the offer

The purchase price is one part of the decision. Ask for property-specific insurance quotes, tax estimates, utility information and any association dues. Include likely maintenance for the roof, equipment, pool, grounds or outbuildings that come with that particular property. Use the Louisiana True Payment Calculator to organize the numbers, then replace estimates with confirmed figures.

For flood questions, start with the address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. Also ask about drainage, prior water intrusion, disclosures and available insurance coverage. A neighborhood label is not a property-specific flood assessment. You can send the address through my Flood Zone Check to start the conversation.

Selling in St. Francisville

A useful pricing conversation starts with comparable properties that buyers could reasonably choose instead of yours. The acreage, condition, layout, location and improvements all matter. Ask how many relevant sales support the recommendation and what changes when the sample is small. A broad median is context. It is not an appraisal of your house.

Before going live, gather the records that help buyers understand the property: surveys, repair documentation, permits, utility details and association information where applicable. Decide which repairs to complete and which to account for in the price. That keeps the preparation tied to the buyer's decision rather than a long list of improvements with no clear purpose.

I will help you build a pricing and marketing plan around the property. Review the estimated proceeds with the Louisiana Seller Net Sheet and talk through your timing, mortgage payoff and next move. For multifamily property, start with accurate operating records and discuss how to share sensitive information with qualified buyers.

What should I check before buying acreage?

Verify the survey, legal access, easements, restrictions, utilities and intended use. Review the condition of any well or wastewater system. Have the appropriate professionals confirm property boundaries, buildability and system requirements before relying on an assumption.

Can I renovate a historic home in St. Francisville?

Start by confirming the property's designation and jurisdiction. Review the town's historic district guidelines and discuss the specific work with the permit office. Get the needed approvals and realistic contractor estimates before committing to the renovation budget.
