# calebjackson.org SEO Audit + Competitor Teardown

Draft for Caleb's review. Nothing published. Data collected live on 2026-10-29 by direct crawl of calebjackson.org (51 pages fetched, all sitemaps parsed, 133 internal links checked) and web search for the three target keywords. The Bright Data CLI had no API key in this session, so collection used direct HTTPS fetches and web search instead. Everything below is labeled: (source) means it came straight from the crawl or SERP, (inference) is my read of it, (not verified) means I could not confirm it.

## Blueprint

How the site works today, step by step:

1. The site is a Luxury Presence build (the consent script loads from cdn.luxurypresence.com) behind Cloudflare, with a custom layer on top: an inline script named `__cjArchitectureMounted` that weighs about 1.5 MB and ships on every page. (source)
2. Content is organized into 21 static pages (buy, sell, tools, calculators, market report), 11 blog posts, 11 neighborhood guides, 40 property pages, and roughly 4,600 IDX home-search URLs under /home-search/. (source: sitemap index, 7 child sitemaps)
3. On-page fundamentals are mostly done right: every page has a unique title, a meta description, a self-referencing canonical, Open Graph tags, and a viewport tag. No page is noindexed. robots.txt is sane and points to the sitemap. (source)
4. Schema is strong on the main site: RealEstateAgent + Organization sitewide, BlogPosting + WebPage on posts, and a rich RealEstateListing + SingleFamilyResidence + Offer + GeoCoordinates stack on property pages. (source)
5. The weight problem: every main page delivers 2.8 to 3.4 MB of raw HTML. On the homepage that breaks down as about 2.24 MB of base64 data URIs (images inlined into the HTML) and 2.76 MB of inline JavaScript, 1.57 MB of it the custom architecture script. Server response is fast (0.45 to 3.8 s to full download), but the browser has to parse all of it before anything is interactive, and the inlined images defeat caching entirely. (source: curl timings and byte analysis; the interactivity cost is inference, not a measured Core Web Vitals score)
6. Heading structure is broken sitewide: main pages carry 4 or 5 H1 tags each, including "Caleb JacksonReal Estate." (the logo) and "Find your place." as H1s on every page, diluting the one H1 that actually targets the page topic. (source)
7. The IDX layer (/home-search/, ~4,600 URLs, the bulk of the site's indexable surface) is the weak half: zero schema markup on search pages and listing pages, listing H1s are just the price ("$449,000"), and the area pages have a template bug that prints the parish ward number into titles and H1s: "Port Hudson, 1 Real Estate & Homes for Sale". Area page meta descriptions are boilerplate ("Explore the beautiful property at Port Hudson, 1..."). (source)
8. Broken links: /profile returns 404 and is linked from 33 of the 35 main pages crawled; /contact-us returns 404 and is linked from property pages; one home-search listing in the on-page link set returns 404 (expired listing left linked). Raw Handlebars template hrefs (`/blog/{{slug}}`, `/neighborhoods/{{slug}}`, `/properties/{{slug}}`) appear in the HTML of most pages. (source)
9. Property page titles are just the street address ("16436 Spanish Ct", 16 characters) with no city, state, or "homes for sale" phrasing, and no "| Caleb Jackson" brand suffix. (source)
10. Image alt coverage is good: only 6 to 8 images per page lack alt text, consistently the same template images. (source)

How the competitors that actually rank work:

11. The three target SERPs are dominated by portals and directories: Zillow, Homes.com, Redfin, Coldwell Banker, RE/MAX, FastExpert, U.S. News, Yelp, effectiveagents.com. Individual agents crack the top results only with specific page types, not homepages. (source: web search, 2026-10-29)
12. Burns & Co. (burnsandco.com) ranks for "Baton Rouge realtor" with its homepage and for St. Francisville with a subdivision-level IDX page ("Bluffs The, Saint Francisville"). Its pages are 171 to 230 KB of HTML, roughly 15x lighter than Caleb's. The subdivision page carries BreadcrumbList + City + Place schema and about 1,450 words of on-page text, plus "Get Instant Alerts on New Listings" capture. The homepage carries AggregateRating schema (star ratings eligible in SERPs) and a SearchAction (sitelinks search box). (source)
13. The W Group ranks for "top real estate agents in Baton Rouge" with a rankings page: a leaderboard of local agents by closed volume, updated monthly. That is a content asset other sites link to and the SERP rewards. Their server blocks automated crawlers (403), so the page-level detail is from the SERP snippet only. (source: SERP snippet; page internals not verified)
14. Property First Realty Group ranks for "Rural homes in St Francisville for sale" with a narrow intent page: a lifestyle-sliced IDX page (rural, acreage, not-in-a-subdivision) with its own explanatory copy, surrounded by sibling pages for condos, foreclosures, land, waterfront, and pools. Homepage is 191 KB with about 3,700 words of indexable text, RealEstateAgent + WebSite schema, city hub pages, monthly market reports, and a blog. Their Darbi McGlone also tops Yelp for Prairieville agents. (source)
15. What they do that calebjackson.org doesn't, distilled: (a) lightweight server-rendered HTML instead of 3 MB payloads; (b) schema on the IDX layer, including BreadcrumbList; (c) intent-sliced area pages (subdivision, rural, waterfront, price band) with real copy on them; (d) review/rating schema that earns stars; (e) a linkable rankings or data asset; (f) a Prairieville presence at all. Caleb's sitemap has no Prairieville, Gonzales, or Ascension Parish neighborhood page despite "Prairieville real estate agent" being a target keyword, and no St. Francisville-specific IDX slice beyond the one neighborhood guide. (source for the gaps: sitemap and page inventory; the strategic framing is inference)

Every static/blog/neighborhood page measured between 2,801 KB and 3,447 KB. Competitor equivalents: burnsandco.com homepage 175,428 B, propertyfirstrealtygroup.com homepage 195,754 B (curl, same day).
- Download times: 0.45 to 3.80 s per page (curl time_total, 2026-10-29); slowest were neighborhoods/st-francisville at 3.80 s and santa-maria at 3.11 s.

## Unknowns

- Real Core Web Vitals (LCP, INP, CLS) and mobile rendering. I measured HTML weight and download time only; I could not run a real browser against the live site in this session or pull field data. The 3 MB payloads make poor mobile scores likely, but that is inference. Run PageSpeed Insights on /, /neighborhoods/st-francisville, and a /home-search/listings page to confirm.
- Whether Google has actually indexed the site and how much of it. The search tool available here ignores the site: operator, so the indexation proxy check failed. A brand search for Caleb Jackson realtor St. Francisville also surfaced nothing from calebjackson.org, which is a worrying signal for brand-query visibility, but it is not proof of an indexing problem. Check Google Search Console coverage, or run site:calebjackson.org in a real browser.
- Whether the `{{slug}}` hrefs are inside inert Handlebars template blocks (harmless to crawlers that execute JS, ugly to ones that don't) or in the live DOM. The pages contain 5 template script blocks, so template-block is the likely answer, but I did not trace each occurrence.
- Rankings beyond what the search tool returned. I got US-sourced results without a Baton Rouge geolocation, so a local searcher's results (map pack included) will differ. The map pack / Google Business Profile picture is entirely unmeasured here, and for "near me" realtor queries it matters more than the organic ten.
- Whether JS injects additional schema on the IDX pages at runtime. My fetches got server HTML; a rendering crawler (this is exactly what Bright Data's layer is for) could confirm. Given the IDX pages are the lightweight server-rendered ones, extra JS-injected schema is unlikely, but unverified.
- Backlink profiles, domain authority, and traffic for the site and competitors. Not measurable with the tools in this session; needs Ahrefs or Semrush.
- The eight 429-limited listing links I did not individually recheck.

## Recommendations (after the facts, in priority order)

1. Fix the two sitewide 404s now: /profile (linked from 33 pages) and /contact-us (linked from property pages). Redirect them to /about and /contact. Smallest fix, immediate crawl-equity win.
2. Attack the page weight: stop inlining images as base64 (2.24 MB on the homepage alone), serve them as cacheable files, and get the 1.5 MB architecture script external, deferred, and split. Target under 500 KB HTML. This is the single biggest technical lever on the site.
3. Fix the IDX layer, because it is 98 percent of your indexable URLs: add BreadcrumbList + RealEstateListing schema, rewrite area-page titles to kill the ", 1" ward bug, and put the address in listing H1s instead of the price.
4. Give property pages real titles: "16436 Spanish Ct, Greenwell Springs LA 70739 | Home for Sale | Caleb Jackson".
5. One H1 per page. Demote the logo and "Find your place." to divs or lower headings.
6. Build what the winners rank with: a Prairieville/Ascension hub page (you target the keyword but have zero Prairieville pages), intent-sliced St. Francisville pages (rural/acreage, under 300K, waterfront) with real copy like PFRG, and consider a linkable data asset like the W Group's leaderboard, which your market-stat blog style is already suited to.
7. Add AggregateRating/Review schema to the testimonials page like Burns & Co., once the reviews on it meet Google's guidelines (real, verifiable, on-page).
8. Confirm the unknowns: PageSpeed Insights on three pages, Search Console coverage, and a Baton Rouge-geolocated SERP check including the map pack.
