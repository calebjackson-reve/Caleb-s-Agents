Part 1

What the video actually says

From the video

  1. What REA is. "Reverse Engineer Anything" is an MCP, a plug-in that connects an AI agent like Claude to older reverse engineering tools (Ghidra, Hopper, IDA) plus Chrome DevTools. The agent asks how an app works, REA pokes at it, and it returns pseudocode (a plain description), proof of what is happening, and unknowns. The agent loops on that until the app is rebuilt.
  2. Setup is easy. He told his agent to download and install it. He runs Claude and Codex side by side in a terminal manager called Orca, but any app works.
  3. The demo. He had one Claude session rebuild the Mac Preview app with REA and a second one rebuild it without. The plain version finished first but felt laggy. The REA version took longer, came out a near match, and drew faster.
  4. It is not new. Ghidra dates to 2019 (from the NSA). Hopper and IDA are much older. REA just gives agents a simple interface to them.
  5. The hype is ahead of the substance. When a video leads with a GitHub-stars chart, it probably lacks something more interesting to show you instead. Show the demo, not the popularity.
  6. What it means for software. Most software trends toward open source and commodity. Wrapper apps get swallowed by the models. The actual value ladder has moved. Cloning to resell hits intellectual property walls. The real change is that recombining existing ideas got cheap.
  7. You may not need REA. Tell your agent you like a feature, ask it to figure out how it works and rebuild it. It will screenshot, study and get close.
  8. His business lens. What new opportunity does this really open versus what doors does this close?

Not verified

His timing claims (plain agents in about two thirds of the time, Claude Code absorbing this within a month or two) are his opinion. The demo is one run on one app, an anecdote rather than a benchmark.

Part 2

Eight takeaways for your business

Applied to you

#His pointWhat it means for you
1The loop matters more than the toolEvery winning reel, listing presentation or follow-up sequence can be taken apart and rebuilt. Make it a habit.
2Pseudocode, proof, unknownsEvery research agent reports as Blueprint, Proof, Unknowns. Nothing gets acted on without proof, and gaps are named, not guessed.
3You often don't need the special toolBefore buying the next AI tool or add-on, ask Claude to rebuild the one feature you actually use.
4Show the demo, not the starsYour GitHub stars are views, likes and followers. Decide on saves, shares, DMs, booked calls and signed agreements.

7Recombination is cheap nowBorrow video formats from outside real estate and apply them to homes and neighborhoods.

Part 3

Six sessions

About 45 to 60 minutes each. Check off what you keep as you go. Checks are saved in this browser only.

01The reverse engineering loop

45 min
Goal
Understand the loop well enough to run it on anything.
Key idea
Ask, take it apart, get a blueprint with proof and unknowns, rebuild, compare, repeat.
Exercise
Pick one thing that worked better than expected in the last 90 days (a post, a listing launch, a client conversation). Write its blueprint, the proof it worked, and what you still don't know about why.

02Teardown lab for video

60 min
Goal
Turn five videos that got real response into reusable blueprints.
Pick five
Three real estate videos from any market and two from outside real estate (food, fitness, finance, home improvement). Choose by comments and shares, not views alone.
Take apart
Run each through the teardown skill:
  • Hook in the first 3 seconds: visual, words, on-screen text
  • The promise that keeps people watching
  • Beats with timestamps, shot types, cuts per 10 seconds
  • On-screen text, audio, length and loop point
  • Call to action and where it lands
  • What people asked or argued about in the comments

03Rebuild and race

60 min
Goal
Turn the blueprints into original Caleb videos.
Exercise
  1. Choose your two strongest blueprints.
  2. Write an original script for each, built on a real listing, a local neighborhood or a question you hear every week.
  3. Write two hooks per script, A and B. This is the two-agent race from the video.
  4. Film both. Post one now and hold the other for a test window.
Formats to borrow
Applied to you
  • Game-show "guess the price" home tour, answer at the end
  • Hot-seat Q and A with a lender or inspector, questions getting harder
  • "Three things I'd change" walkthrough, with seller permission
  • Before and after of staging or a renovation
  • Walk-and-talk on one market stat, with the source on screen

04Reverse engineer your own wins

45 min
Goal
Decompile your best work into a system an agent can run.
Exercise
Take your smoothest closed deal from the last year. Walk it from first contact to closing gift. For each step write what you did, the tool, what you said and how long it took. Then mark it:
  • Me only: relationship or judgment
  • Agent can draft: I approve before it goes out
  • Agent can run: internal only, no approval needed

05Tool stack audit and the AIRE moat

60 min
Stack audit
List every paid tool and the one or two features you actually use. Sort each:
  • Core: holds data, compliance or a network you can't rebuild (CRM of record, MLS, e-signature, transaction management)
  • Rebuildable: Claude could build the feature you use (open house sign-in, buyer consultation one-pager, a seller net sheet estimate checked against title or escrow)
  • Redundant: another tool already does it
Build the replacement first, run both for 30 days, then decide. Cancel nothing during the lesson.
Moat test
Applied to you For every AIRE feature or pitch, ask: If Claude shipped this as a built-in next month, would anyone still need it from me? Likely yes when it runs on your data, relationships, local judgment, voice or a workflow with accountability. Likely no for a generic caption writer, description generator or chat wrapper.

06Hype filter and review

45 min
Goal
Judge by outcomes, then lock in what worked.
Exercise
Compare the Session 3 videos to your 30-day Metricool baseline using the scorecard below. Keep blueprints that moved value metrics. Retire ones that only moved vanity metrics.
Hype filter
For any new AI tool or trend:
  1. Is there a real demo, or only a popularity chart?
  2. Can plain Claude do 80 percent of it today?
  3. Does it open a door for my business or close one?
  4. What would I stop doing if I adopted it?

  • Likes

  • Trending sounds used

Value: decide on it

Part 6

Guardrails

  • Copy the mechanics, not the content. Study structure and format. Never reuse another creator's footage, music, script wording, thumbnails or branding.
  • Listing media belongs to someone. Don't reuse another agent's listing photos or video. MLS rules and photographer copyright apply.
  • Software licenses. Many forbid reverse engineering, and he flagged IP risk himself. Rebuild from visible behavior for internal use. Ask an attorney when unclear.
  • Competitor funnels. Walk public pages as a visitor. No fake leads or showing requests.
  • Advertising rules. Follow your state's rules and Keller Williams First Choice brokerage disclosure requirements. Keep claims accurate. Apply Fair Housing to content and targeting.
  • Sourced numbers only. Every market stat shows its source and date.
  • Your approval gate. Agents draft. You approve before anything is posted, sent, published or paid for.

Appendix

Copy-paste teardown prompt

For any Claude session where the skill isn't loaded.

Prompt
Tear this down for me: [link, transcript or screenshots]

Why I picked it: [what result it got]
What I want to rebuild: [a video for my listing at ___ / a page / a sequence]

Report back in three parts.
1. Blueprint: how it works, step by step, in plain words. For video include hook,
   promise, beats with timestamps, pacing, on-screen text, audio, length and CTA.
2. Proof: the evidence for each part (timestamps, quotes, metrics, comments).
3. Unknowns: what you can't tell from what I gave you.

Then write two original versions (A and B) for my business as a REALTOR with
Keller Williams First Choice. Copy the mechanics, not the content. Use my voice:
warm, direct and natural, no em dashes, no semicolons, no corporate polish.
Don't post or send anything.
