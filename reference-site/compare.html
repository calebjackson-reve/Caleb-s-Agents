How the numbers work

What is calculated

  • Loan payment: a fixed-rate amortizing payment on price minus down payment.
  • Mortgage insurance: only when the down payment is under 20%, at the yearly rate you enter. Your lender sets the real figure.
  • Taxes and insurance: your yearly amounts divided by 12.
  • Upkeep reserve: a yearly percentage of the price you choose, set aside for repairs. It is a planning allowance, not an estimate of this home’s condition.
  • Cash to close: down payment + closing costs − credits.
  • Biggest difference: the cost line that accounts for most of the monthly gap between the lowest and highest home.

Louisiana specifics

  • Residential property is assessed at 10% of market value. A homestead exemption removes up to $7,500 of assessed value ($75,000 of market value) from most, but not all, millages. Your parish assessor publishes millage rates. Rule as quoted in HB 440 (2026), legis.la.gov. Bills to raise the exemption died in committee in April 2026.
  • A seller’s current tax bill can change after a sale, and the seller’s homestead exemption does not carry over to you until you file your own. Louisiana taxes are billed late in the year and due December 31 for that same year, so your closing statement shows how the year’s tax is split.
  • Look up the flood zone for an address on FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center. The zone is not a premium; get a flood quote for the address.
  • Insurance share of payment: Realtor.com data from mid-August 2025, as reported by Louisiana Radio Network, put Louisiana highest of any state at 18.2%. A statewide average, not a figure for any home.
  • Your lender’s Loan Estimate is the document to use for closing costs and cash to close.

What this does not do

  • It does not value homes, predict prices or rank neighborhoods.
  • It does not fetch live listings, taxes or insurance quotes.
  • It does not tell you which home to buy. It shows what each one would cost you.
  • It excludes utilities, commuting and any cost you did not enter.
